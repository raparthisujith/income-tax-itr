A few more things to verify

Flags only — not computed by this tool
→ Foreign assets/accounts (Schedule FA): if you (or Avanthi) hold any foreign bank account, brokerage, ESOP, or directorship abroad at any point in the year, disclosure is mandatory regardless of value — penalties under the Black Money Act are severe and separate from income tax penalties.
→ Partner in a firm (Schedule IF): if Healup or any other firm pays you remuneration/interest as a partner, that's reported separately from this business income, and since FY 2025-26 firms must deduct TDS at 10% on partner remuneration over ₹20,000/year under Sec 194T — check that's been credited.
→ Books of account (Sec 44AA): if you're not using presumptive taxation, income over ₹2.5L or turnover over ₹25L in any of the last 3 years means you're required to maintain regular books — not just this tracker, but statutory books your CA can certify.
→ 26AS/AIS reconciliation: the TDS reconciliation tool further down only checks what you upload against what you've logged — it doesn't replace actually opening your AIS/26AS on the portal before filing.
SEC 44AD/44ADA

Presumptive taxation check

Auto-picks whichever computes lower tax, per regime

A specified profession (legal, medical, engineering, architecture, accountancy, technical consultancy, interior decoration, and a few notified others) can declare 50% of gross receipts as taxable profit under Sec 44ADA, up to ₹50L receipts (₹75L if mostly digital). An eligible business can declare 6% (digital) or 8% (cash) of turnover under Sec 44AD instead, up to ₹2cr (₹3cr if mostly digital) — but not businesses earning commission/brokerage, or leasing goods carriages. Neither is "tax-free income" — it's a deemed minimum profit that's still fully taxed. It only helps if your real expenses run above what the deemed rate implies; if your actual books already show a lower profit, that beats presumptive taxation on its own.

Result

Tax payable, computed both ways

Basis: Finance Act 2025 rates for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27) — new regime slabs of ₹0–4L nil, 5%/10%/15%/20%/25%/30% thereafter with a ₹75,000 standard deduction and Section 87A rebate up to ₹60,000 (nil tax to ₹12L); old regime unchanged with a ₹50,000 standard deduction and ₹12,500 rebate up to ₹5L. Health & education cess of 4% applies to both. Surcharge (10%/15%/25%, capped at 25% in the new regime) is applied where total income exceeds ₹50L, without marginal relief — near a threshold, actual tax may be a little lower than shown. Section 87A rebate is applied only against tax on normal-rate income, not against Sec 111A/112A capital gains tax. Under the new regime a house-property loss cannot be set off against other heads, and Chapter VI-A deductions (other than 80CCD(2)/80JJAA) aren't available at all. Section 80GG and 80D caps are simplified. This sheet is for planning only — it isn't tax advice, and doesn't replace a CA's sign-off on your actual return. Taxes already paid: TDS logged against an income entry, other TDS (salary/interest/rent), advance tax instalments, and self-assessment tax are netted off the total liability to show your balance payable or refund. If less than 90% of the year's tax is paid by 31 March, or an instalment is short, interest under Sections 234B/234C can apply — this sheet doesn't compute that interest, so a shortfall near year-end is worth flagging to your CA.
